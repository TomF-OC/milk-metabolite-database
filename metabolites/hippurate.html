About Hippurate

Chemical class
Aromatic nitrogenous compound
Biochemical description
Hippurate is conjugate of benzoic acid and glycine. Hippurate originates from microbial degradation of dietary aromatic compounds such as chlorogenic and caffeoylquinic acids found in fresh forage.
Relevance in milk and feeding systems · diet study
Hippurate was the most distinctive metabolite identified in relation to dietary regimen, showing a strong proportional response to the amount of pasture in the cow's diet. Hippurate concentrations were significantly influenced by feeding systems (p < 0.001), with higher concentrations in milk from GRS diets, followed by PMR, and with TMR diets with the lowest concentrations. In the partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA), hippurate exhibited a variable importance in projection (VIP) score higher than 2.5 and in the biomarker analysis comparing GRS vs. TMR, it had an area under the curve (AUC) of 1.0, indicating excellent discriminatory power.
Seasonal trend · seasonality study
Hippurate concentrations were significantly influenced by season (p < 0.001), with higher concentrations in spring and summer and lower in winter and autumn.
